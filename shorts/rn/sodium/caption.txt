Hyponatremia vs hypernatremia: why slow and steady matters 🧠
Normal sodium is about 135–145 mEq/L (ranges vary by lab). Water follows sodium.
Low Na: water shifts into brain cells → swelling (cerebral edema): headache, confusion, seizures.
High Na: water leaves the cells → they shrink: thirst, confusion, seizures.
Correct chronic hyponatremia slowly (a common limit is about 10 mEq/L in 24 h, 8 or less in high-risk patients) to avoid osmotic demyelination. Severe symptoms: 3% hypertonic saline per protocol. Lower high sodium gradually too.
Neuro checks, seizure precautions, strict I&O, frequent sodium checks.
