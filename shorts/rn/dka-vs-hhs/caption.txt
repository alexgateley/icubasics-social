DKA vs HHS: the storm and the drought ⛈️
DKA: hours to a few days, more common in younger people with type 1. Too little insulin, so fat burns and ketones make the blood acidic. Deep, rapid breathing, fruity breath, vomiting. Glucose usually 200 or more, pH under 7.30 or bicarb under 18.
HHS: days to weeks, more common in older adults with type 2. A little insulin limits ketones while sugar climbs and the patient dries out. Glucose 600 or more, effective osmolality over 300, no big ketones, often confused.
Both: fluids first. Check potassium before insulin, because insulin pushes potassium into cells.
