Calcium, phosphorus and magnesium: three small numbers that can stop a heart ⚡
Calcium and phosphorus tend to move in opposite directions: in kidney failure, phosphorus climbs and calcium falls. PTH and vitamin D pull calcium back up.
Low calcium: tingling, tetany, Chvostek and Trousseau signs, a long QT, seizures. Normal total calcium is about 8.8–10.3 mg/dL (ranges vary by lab).
Low albumin makes total calcium read low. Corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin), but ionized calcium is more reliable.
Low magnesium keeps potassium and calcium low, so replace magnesium along with them. IV magnesium treats torsades.
Too much magnesium: reflexes fade first, then breathing can stop. Hold the magnesium; IV calcium gluconate counters its effects (per protocol).
Refeeding a starved patient can crash phosphorus.
