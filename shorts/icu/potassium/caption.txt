High K or low K: what each one does to the heart 🫀
Hyperkalemia: peaked T waves → widening QRS → sine wave. Monitor, calcium gluconate to stabilize the membrane (K unchanged), insulin + dextrose to shift K into cells, then watch the glucose.
Hypokalemia: flat T waves, U waves, ST depression. Check magnesium; low Mg makes K hard to correct.
Never IV push potassium: dilute, pump, within your policy's max rate.
