Isotonic vs hypotonic vs hypertonic: where does the water go? 💧
Water moves toward more particles.
Isotonic (normal saline, lactated Ringer's): no shift, cells keep their size. Your shock fluid.
Hypotonic (half normal saline): water moves into cells and they swell. Free water for high sodium. Never for shock, never with a swollen brain.
The trap: D5W reads isotonic in the bag (about 250 mOsm/L), but the body burns the sugar and leaves water. It acts hypotonic.
Hypertonic (3% saline): water leaves cells and they shrink. For severe low sodium or raised intracranial pressure: slowly, per protocol, through a big vein. Too fast in chronic low sodium risks osmotic demyelination.
